# Whip Pan

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Whip Pan
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** camera pans so fast the frame smears into streaks; landing on a new subject, or cutting mid-smear to another whip, converts velocity itself into the edit.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Impact transitions between scenes/locations at high energy (sports, fashion runway beats, chase grammar).
- Escalation: consecutive whips accelerate a sequence's rhythm.
- Comedy/surprise reveals: whip lands on something unexpected.
- Location jump without match-cut logic — pure kinetic glue.

## Avoid When

- Elegance/premium calm contexts: smear reads cheap in luxury minimalism unless deliberately contrasted.
- Identity-critical reveal ON the landing frame if generation smears the settle — faces must resolve crisply.
- More than ~3 whips consecutively: motion sickness + diminishing returns.
- Slow narrative beats that need audience settling time.

## Required Inputs

- Exit subject and entry subject (whip needs an A and B).
- Whip direction (left/right/up) consistent with screen-direction logic.
- Landing composition for B prepared before the move starts.
- Rhythm slot: where on the audio beat the whip fires.

## Directing Rule

- Fire the whip on the beat: start acceleration exactly at musical impact.
- Peak blur occupies the middle ~40% of travel; A exits frame by 25% travel, B enters by 75%.
- Direction discipline: same-direction whips chain energy; alternating directions reset it — choose per rhythm map.
- Land B with a settle: tiny overshoot-and-correct (~5% of travel) makes landing physical rather than digital.
- Cut-in-the-blur option: two shots each whipping the same direction, joined at full smear — the smear hides the seam entirely.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** A composed normally; motion arrow annotation showing whip exit direction.
- **Transition frame:** the smear frame itself — horizontal streak field, no legible subject; board it as a designed abstract.
- **Reveal frame:** B landed and settled at its final composition (already key-art ready).

## Image Generation Translation

Generate the landing frame as primary key art (B crisp). If a smear still is needed: directional motion blur, horizontal light streaks, zero readable faces — use only as texture interstitial, never as hero image.

## Video Generation Translation

Specify: start framing on A, whip direction, total duration (0.3–0.6s typical), blur behavior ("frame smears to directional streaks at midpoint"), landing target B with exact final composition, settle micro-correction. For cut-in-blur variant: declare both shots' identical direction and the join point at maximum smear.

## Failure Modes

- Smear never resolves (B stays soft) → demand "sharp focus locked within final 10% of move."
- Direction flip mid-whip (generation wobbles) → one axis, one direction, short duration.
- Landing recomposes (B drifts after arrival) → freeze declaration: "final composition holds ≥1s."
- Overuse flattens rhythm → alternate with static or slow shots; log whip usage per sequence.

## Preservation Rules

Faces resolve fully sharp at landing before any further action. Product/logo: land them centered and undistorted; whip INTO product frames, never past them mid-frame at readable speed. Screen direction preserved across chained whips. Motif link: motif may ride the smear (color streak of the motif palette) or be revealed BY the whip; verb-Match via direction continuity. Downgrade path: crash-cut (see crash-transition.md) when smear generation is unreliable.
