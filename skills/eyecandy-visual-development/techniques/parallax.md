# Parallax

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Parallax
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** camera movement across layered depth makes near planes slide fast and far planes crawl — space itself becomes the event, revealing scale and structure that a static frame hides.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Spatial expansion: flat-looking scenes must prove their depth (interiors, cityscapes, product environments).
- Reveal by geometry: subject hidden behind a near-plane object slides into view as camera tracks.
- Elegance: slow lateral drift gives stillness a heartbeat without cutting.
- Key visual to motion conversion: one hero image re-staged as layers for a living frame.

## Avoid When

- The scene has no genuine depth lanes — parallax over a flat backdrop reads as cardboard cutout error.
- Fast rhythm sections: parallax is inherently unhurried; forcing it into quick cuts wastes it.
- Depth-of-field confusion in generation produces double-exposure ghosts instead of clean layer separation.
- Subject must dominate attention; strong parallax competes with faces.

## Required Inputs

- Minimum three depth planes with distinct content (foreground frame element / midground subject / background world).
- Camera path: lateral track, arc segment, or push-in with offset.
- Relative speeds implied by distances (near fast, far slow).
- What the movement reveals or connects at each end of the path.

## Directing Rule

- Stage a foreground framing element deliberately (branch, pillar, shoulder, product edge) even if the base scene lacks one — parallax needs its near anchor.
- Move camera laterally or on a shallow arc; keep focal distance locked so planes separate purely by displacement speed.
- Subject stays on its plane, crossing behind/in front of the near anchor during travel — this occlusion-and-release IS the beat.
- Travel distance sized so at least one full occlusion-and-release occurs per shot.
- End the move on composition, not exhaustion: final frame must be key-art balanced.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** subject partially behind near-plane anchor; only sliver visible — curiosity armed.
- **Transition frame:** mid-track — maximum separation between plane speeds, subject emerging from behind the anchor's edge.
- **Reveal frame:** subject fully clear of anchor against full background context; near anchor now frames the opposite edge.

## Image Generation Translation

Compose with aggressive foreground intrusion: anchor element large, close, possibly soft-edged but recognizable; subject mid-frame fully sharp; deep background legible. The still must imply "camera is about to slide" via strong diagonal layer boundaries. This is the standard key-art-with-depth recipe.

## Video Generation Translation

Specify: direction and length of camera travel, three planes and their contents, lock on subject focus. Declare relative displacement qualitatively ("near element crosses frame twice as fast as subject shifts; background nearly static"). Duration 3–6s typical. No zoom during lateral move unless intent is combined dolly-zoom grammar (separate technique).

## Failure Modes

- Layer tearing: planes render as flat stickers sliding independently → declare shared lighting and atmospheric haze between planes to bind them.
- Subject swims off its plane (changes size during track) → lock subject-to-camera distance.
- Foreground anchor becomes the subject (audience watches the branch) → keep anchor ≤40% frame area and visually subordinate.
- Infinite smoothness with nothing revealed → schedule the occlusion beat explicitly in time ("subject clears pillar at ~60% duration").

## Preservation Rules

Subject identity: face never more than half-covered by the anchor at any instant. Product/logo surfaces stay perpendicular-ish to camera through the move (skew reads cheap). Wardrobe/props persist. Spatial continuity: plane order (what is in front of what) declared once and never violated. Motif link: place motif as the near-plane anchor so the world literally passes behind it, or as the revealed subject — verb-Match or verb-Occlusion per motif-system-link.md. Downgrade path: static frame + subject walk-behind achieves mini-parallax when camera travel generation is unstable.
