# Silhouette

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Silhouette
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** subject rendered as pure outline against brighter ground — identity reduced to shape, meaning carried by contour, mystery by concealment.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Isolation: strip context and detail; leave only essential form.
- Visual hook: bold graphic poster frames (black figure, huge sun).
- Tension/mystery: withhold face until the story earns it.
- Motif-linkage: silhouettes are the safest cross-scene match currency (shape survives any re-lighting/re-location).

## Avoid When

- Product hero shots requiring label/form detail (silhouette hides exactly what commerce needs).
- Face reveal IS imminent in the same shot (wasted concealment).
- Low subject/background contrast — muddy half-silhouette reads as exposure error.
- Entire film silhouetted: value structure collapses; use as accent grammar.

## Required Inputs

- Subject with a STRONG readable outline (distinctive hair, garment volume, product profile, pose).
- Bright ground source: sky, window, doorway, screen, light wall, practical lamp field.
- Exposure logic: subject underexposed ≥2 stops vs ground.
- Pose direction: which contour tells the story (hand raised, mid-stride, profile).

## Directing Rule

- Place light source directly behind or beyond subject; camera sees the shadow side only.
- Contour is script: direct the POSE first — every limb must read unambiguously against ground (no merged arm-torso blobs).
- Frame geometry: subject occupies 15–60% of frame height typically; extreme smallness = loneliness, largeness = monument.
- Ground content stays simple: one bright mass, minimal internal detail to compete with contour.
- Color option: duotone discipline — silhouette near-black, ground carries the palette; motif colors live in the GROUND.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** bright ground established alone (empty doorway/sky) — stage armed.
- **Transition frame:** subject entering as growing black form — outline partially formed, curiosity engaged.
- **Reveal frame:** full iconic silhouette composition held as key art; OR anti-climax reveal: subject steps through and lighting flips to show detail (the payoff cut).

## Image Generation Translation

Recipe: high-contrast rim composition — subject solid dark mass with crisp edges, luminous simple background, no fill on subject side. Verify: outline gap-free, pose legible, single dominant ground mass. This yields reliably striking key art with zero identity-rendering risk (useful when face fidelity is uncertain).

## Video Generation Translation

Declare: backlight source and position, subject pose and its evolution (one gesture arc), exposure relationship ("subject renders as solid dark shape; background remains bright and simple"), camera behavior (static for graphic purity, or slow lateral for parallax between subject edge and ground). Duration 2–5s; end on hold.

## Failure Modes

- Half-lit faces (generation adds accidental fill) → explicitly forbid facial detail/light on subject plane.
- Contour melt: limbs merge into body mass → widen pose; separate limbs from torso by visible gaps of ground.
- Background busyness competes → simplify ground declaration; move detail sources out of frame.
- Edge fringing/halo artifacts around outline → avoid high-saturation grounds directly behind head region; shift ground hue away from skin-tone band.

## Preservation Rules

Identity strategy: silhouette CONCEALS identity by design — sequence must plan when identity returns (never leave audience unable to verify who they watched). If the silhouette IS a known character, keep signature contour features (hair shape, garment line) accurate. Product: silhouette allowed ONLY for teaser phases; purchase-intent frames restore full detail. Wardrobe: silhouette flattens texture — carry wardrobe continuity cues via cut, not within silhouette shots. Motif link: strongest verb-Match carrier — silhouette shapes match-cut across scenes flawlessly. Downgrade path: heavy backlight with 10% facial detail ("veiled" look) when total silhouette blocks narrative need.
