# Central Framing

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Central Framing
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** subject placed dead-center with symmetric or radial support — iconographic, confrontational, timeless; the frame becomes a shrine.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Visual hook: opening frames that must read as posters/icons.
- Elegance/premium: symmetry signals control and confidence (luxury baseline grammar).
- Isolation by geometry: centered subject in vast negative space.
- Motif emphasis: circular/radial motifs demand center placement to complete.

## Avoid When

- Dialogue coverage needing over-shoulder geography — center framing fights shot-reverse-shot flow.
- Multiple subjects of equal narrative weight (center holds ONE master).
- Every single shot of a film (symmetry fatigue; contrast rhythm dies).
- Dynamic chase/action beats requiring lead room.

## Required Inputs

- Single hero subject (person, product, or architectural moment).
- Symmetry axis source: architecture (corridor, arch), lighting (center pool), subject's own bilateral form.
- Negative space budget: how much emptiness surrounds the hero.
- Vertical anchor: eye-line height / product horizon line.

## Directing Rule

- Subject center = frame center within 2% tolerance; declare vertical placement explicitly (eyes at upper-third line for faces; label at lower-center for products).
- Build supporting geometry symmetrically: left element mirrors right element in mass and value (not necessarily identical content).
- Camera on-axis only: zero dutch, minimal perspective skew; frontal or perfectly orthogonal views.
- Background depth must recede symmetrically (corridor vanishing point behind subject) OR stay void-flat — no busy asymmetry behind the hero.
- For sequences: alternate central shots with asymmetric shots to restore the device's power.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** environment establishing its axis WITHOUT subject (empty corridor/pool of light) — arms the stage.
- **Transition frame:** subject arriving INTO the axis (mid-step toward center) — motion toward the shrine.
- **Reveal frame:** full iconic composition — subject locked to center, symmetry complete. This frame is the key art deliverable.

## Image Generation Translation

The signature still: perfect bilateral symmetry, subject dead-center, vanishing point aligned directly behind them, controlled palette. Enforce: no cropped limbs at frame edge, mirrored background masses, horizon/eye-line declared. This is the most reliable "poster frame" recipe in this library.

## Video Generation Translation

Static or minimal dolly-in along the symmetry axis ("camera pushes straight toward centered subject; all background lines converge to the point behind their head/label"). Declare hold duration at full composition. Any lateral move destroys the device — forbid drift.

## Failure Modes

- Near-symmetry (almost-centered reads as error, not style) → enforce exact centering; check both halves for mass balance.
- Perspective skew from generation lens quirks → declare ortho-frontal view.
- Subject micro-drift during push-ins → lock framing declaration; short durations.
- Symmetric clutter: too many mirrored elements compete → limit to one axis system per frame.

## Preservation Rules

Face: frontal-centered portraits are identity-critical — verify likeness at final framing. Product label: centered means READABLE — enforce label plane perpendicular to camera, unobstructed, undistorted. Logo: may BE the symmetry anchor (mark as centerpiece). Wardrobe symmetry itself can be a design feature (declare intentional asymmetry if garment has it). Motif link: radial/circular motifs complete ONLY under central framing — coordinate verb-Match. Downgrade path: none needed — this is the most generation-stable technique in the library; failures indicate prompt-level issues elsewhere.
