# Crash Cut

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Crash cut (registry slug crash-transition)
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** violent hard cut between maximally different frames — loud/quiet, bright/dark, wide/tight — the collision itself generates impact; nothing survives the seam on purpose.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Impact puncts: beat drops, reveals-that-shock, scene-ending exclamation marks.
- Escalation ladders: each crash cut raises energy/state distance from the previous frame.
- Contrast rhetoric: wealth→poverty, calm→chaos — meaning through collision.
- Rhythm resets after long smooth sequences.

## Avoid When

- Anywhere continuity is the message (crash cuts declare "world break" — don't use inside continuous geography without intent).
- Identity-critical comparison moments (audience must verify sameness → use match-cut instead).
- More than 3 consecutive crashes without a breather shot (numbing).
- Brand-serene contexts unless the brand voice is deliberately aggressive.

## Required Inputs

- Frame A and Frame B with declared contrast axes (pick 2+: scale, brightness, density, sound level, color temperature, motion).
- Beat placement: crash lands ON accent, never before it.
- Sequence context: what the audience's eye expects (crash works by violating expectation).

## Directing Rule

- Design A's LAST frame and B's FIRST frame as deliberate opposites on chosen axes; write the pair as a contract ("A: wide daylight empty / B: macro night crowded").
- No transitional devices: no whip, no blur bridge, no sound overlap smearing the seam — the hardness IS the device. Clean frame A → clean frame B.
- B opens already at full intensity (no ramp-in) — arrival at maximum is what crashes.
- Audio discipline: cut audio exactly with picture; a silent frame before impact amplifies it (micro-gap technique).
- After the crash, hold B ≥1s so the collision registers before next motion.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** A at its most resolved, stable composition — calm before collision must be REAL calm.
- **Transition frame:** the cut line itself; board as paired stills with contrast axes annotated.
- **Reveal frame:** B at full intensity composition, held.

## Image Generation Translation

Generate the pair (A-still + B-still) under the contrast contract; both individually key-art grade. The B-still usually becomes the sequence's poster frame since it owns maximum energy.

## Video Generation Translation

Two independent shots, hard join. Declare per shot: content, dominant axes values, end/start states. Forbid: crossfade, flash-frame bridges, motion handoff. Duration guidance: A ends at natural rest or mid-action (both valid — declare which), B starts at action peak.

## Failure Modes

- Accidental soft seam (generation adds blend frames at join) → assemble externally; verify zero interpolated frames at the boundary.
- Weak contrast (B too similar to A) → re-check axis table; strengthen at least two axes.
- Crash fatigue → alternate energy levels across sequence; log every crash position in the rhythm map.
- B wobbles on arrival (generation settling artifacts) → demand locked first second of B.

## Preservation Rules

Faces/products appear fully formed in B's first frame — crash cuts never catch subjects mid-morph. Brand assets: if logo reveal rides the crash, it lands complete and static for its hold duration. Continuity of character state (wardrobe, injuries, props) remains TRUE even though visuals collide — contrast is presentation-level, not canon-level, unless narrative declares otherwise. Motif link: deliberately NOT a motif carrier (that's match-cut's job); if motif appears in both A and B it must be transformed, else the crash reads as failed match-cut. Downgrade path: whip-pan when pure hardness feels abrupt for the brand register.
