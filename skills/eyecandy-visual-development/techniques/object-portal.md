# Object Portal

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Object Portal
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — treat identity below as derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** an object's opening or outline becomes a doorway — camera passes through it and another world is inside, collapsing object-scale into space-scale.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Product film: the product contains its own universe (bottle mouth → ocean interior).
- Key visual needs one image to promise two worlds (object shell + inner world visible through it).
- Scene transition must hide a location change inside a familiar object.
- Escalation ladder: motif grows until its interior becomes habitable space.

## Avoid When

- The object's interior contradicts product truth (drink bottle shown containing a desert can mislead) — brand claims outrank poetry.
- Portal shape is unclear; ambiguous openings read as rendering errors.
- Two portals in one sequence without escalation between them.
- Fast-paced edits: portal reveals need dwell time to land.

## Required Inputs

- Portal object with a legible opening (ring, mouth, window, arch, hollow handle).
- Interior world distinct in palette/depth from exterior.
- Pass direction (forward through, pull-back out).
- Scale logic: object size in exterior world vs. perceived interior volume.

## Directing Rule

- Frame the portal dead-center or on a strong third; its opening defines an inner frame within the outer frame.
- Camera approaches the opening on the portal's optical axis; deviation >15° breaks the "window" read.
- Exterior lighting must rim the portal edges so the opening reads darker/brighter than surroundings — the eye needs a threshold.
- On crossing: interior world inherits the portal shape as its first visible geometry (arch becomes corridor curve), then expands.
- Scale contradiction is the point: small object outside, vast space inside. Do not reconcile it.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** object established in its exterior context at true scale; opening facing camera or turning toward it.
- **Transition frame:** camera at the threshold — opening fills most of frame, interior world faintly visible, exterior edges still present.
- **Reveal frame:** fully inside; portal shape echoes in interior architecture; origin object no longer visible.

## Image Generation Translation

One keyframe at threshold: concentric framing — outer object silhouette, inner world glowing through the opening, high contrast between the two zones. Keep the object's brand surfaces undistorted at the frame edges. This single image must work as standalone key art.

## Video Generation Translation

Continuous forward move along portal axis: approach (exterior scale cues visible) → threshold crossing (edges wipe past frame border) → interior expansion (new world revealed with portal-shaped geometry). Declare speed profile: slow approach, brief acceleration through crossing, settle inside. No cut; one take.

## Failure Modes

- Interior/exterior blend: model averages palettes at crossing → enforce hard lighting change at threshold.
- Portal geometry melts mid-pass (rings warp) → keep the pass short; re-anchor shape in prompt at crossing.
- Object identity loss: brand surface warps as camera nears → cap approach so logo-bearing faces stay planar, or cross through a non-branded opening.
- Inner world too literal (mini copy of exterior) → declare interior's distinct physics/palette explicitly.

## Preservation Rules

Product form and label survive untouched up to the threshold frame — crossings route through openings, never through printed surfaces. Face identity: if a character leads the pass, they enter first and remain lit separately from the world change. Motif continuity: portal shape should derive from the active motif (see motif-system-link.md verb: Occlusion). Downgrade path: replace pass-through with a match-cut from portal opening to similarly shaped interior architecture.
