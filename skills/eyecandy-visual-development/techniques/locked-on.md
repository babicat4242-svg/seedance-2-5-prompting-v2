# Locked-On

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Locked-On
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** camera rigidly welded to the subject — subject frame-stable while the WORLD moves around them — producing unnatural, magnetic presence (the world orbits them; they do not move).

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Isolation with power: character walks through chaos/party/battleframe and remains perfectly framed.
- Tension: stillness-as-menace — approaching subject never bobs or jitters.
- Impact: entrance shots where composure itself is the flex.
- Product: carrier-mounted product glide through environment (form never wobbles).

## Avoid When

- Documentary realism contexts (locked-on reads stylized/constructed).
- Weak performers/faces that need motion for life — total stability exposes stiffness in generation.
- Long durations: >6–8s of lock without environmental payoff becomes dead air.
- Scenes requiring camera-as-observer empathy (lock makes camera a character).

## Required Inputs

- Subject with strong silhouette/walk/glide (the device shows off their constancy).
- Environment with motion content: crowd, debris, passing lights, terrain.
- Mount logic: rig on subject vs. virtual track (declare internally; output stays model-agnostic).
- Framing choice: centered symmetrical (iconic/menace) vs rule-of-thirds (traveling lead room).

## Directing Rule

- Subject occupies a FIXED screen region for the entire shot — declare exact region and hold it.
- All expressiveness transfers to the environment: it must move fast enough to prove the lock (parallax streaks, people swarming past).
- Gait/pace constant; background speed communicates velocity instead of camera bounce.
- Center-framing maximizes menace/icon status; off-center adds narrative direction (where they're headed).
- End by breaking or honoring the lock deliberately: subject finally stops (world catches up) OR camera releases to handheld (world wins).

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** subject in locked framing at entry point; environment already alive behind.
- **Transition frame:** mid-travel — subject pixel-identical position, background composition fully changed (the proof pair).
- **Reveal frame:** arrival — either lock breaks (release move) or destination recontextualizes the stillness.

## Image Generation Translation

Single frame: subject tack-sharp, centered per spec, environment motion-blurred around them (crowd streaks, light trails). The contrast sharp-subject/blurred-world IS the technique's key art. Avoid blur on the subject's edges.

## Video Generation Translation

Specify: "subject holds exact frame position [region] for full duration while environment moves past at high relative speed; camera translates smoothly with zero shake; subject gait steady." Declare duration 3–6s, environment contents and their motion directions, and the end-of-lock behavior.

## Failure Modes

- Subject drifts within frame (imperfect tracking) → shorten duration; simplify path to straight line.
- Environment too static (lock unproven, looks like tripod) → add explicit moving elements and their speeds.
- Subject motion-cancels (walks backward etc.) confusing the physics → one locomotion verb only.
- Face micro-morphing over long lock → keep ≤5s or cut to re-establish.

## Preservation Rules

Face identity under maximum scrutiny — long stable close framings expose every drift; cap duration and verify first/last frame likeness. Product form: ideal technique for product IF surface reflections stay coherent (moving environment reflects in glossy surfaces — declare reflection behavior). Wardrobe physics: fabric should respond to travel (wind cue) else the lock reads frozen. Motif link: motif elements populate the rushing background (verb-Occlusion as they flash past) or sit ON the subject unchanged. Downgrade path: static camera + world moving through frame achieves 70% of the read when tracking fails.
