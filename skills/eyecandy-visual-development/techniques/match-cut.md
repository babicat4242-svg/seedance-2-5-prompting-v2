# Match Cut

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Match Cut
- **Source status:** page-definition. Official site definition (verbatim): "…the match cut technique comes in and seamlessly links two completely different scenes just to mess with your head." (witty register; the operative fact: it links two different scenes seamlessly.)
- **Visual purpose:** one visual property survives a cut into an unrelated scene — shape, position, motion, color, light, texture, scale, silhouette, or gesture — welding two worlds into one thought.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Motif linkage: the active motif must travel between scenes that cannot be shot as one space.
- Conceptual rhyme: two scenes share meaning, not location (bone → satellite).
- Visual hook: open on A, cut to B so the audience learns the film's visual language in one edit.
- Compression: skip dead travel time by jumping on a matched property.

## Avoid When

- The matched property is weak or coincidental — audiences must recognize it within ~6 frames.
- Both scenes are already visually similar; the cut then reads as an error, not wit.
- Every scene uses one (see intent-routing anti-forcing rules) — match cuts stop being intelligent when habitual.
- Identity-critical subject changes appearance across the cut while also carrying the match — split the jobs.

## Required Inputs

- Outgoing scene's strongest property (name it: shape/position/motion/color/light/texture/scale/silhouette/gesture).
- Incoming scene built around that same property.
- Frame position of the property in scene A (it should reappear at the same screen position in scene B).

## Directing Rule

- Design the outgoing frame so the matching element occupies a strong, isolated position (center, third-point, or full-frame dominance).
- The incoming frame opens with that element at the SAME screen position and roughly similar apparent size — then the new scene diverges from there.
- Match ONE primary property; secondary properties may contrast (color matches while scale flips for surprise).
- Cut lands on a beat (audio or action apex), not mid-drift.
- After the cut, camera behavior in scene B should extend scene A's motion vector when possible — continuity of energy sells the lie.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame (end of scene A):** matching element at its strongest framing; everything else in scene A resolved enough to leave.
- **Transition frame:** THE CUT ITSELF is the transition frame — board it as a pair: "A-last: circle at center / B-first: circle at center."
- **Reveal frame (scene B established):** new world fully legible; the survived element now integrated into B's logic.

## Image Generation Translation

Generate the pair as a diptych discipline: image A with the motif element dominant and positioned; image B opening composition with the same element at same position/scale but transformed context. Each still works alone; side by side they prove the match. For key art, image B is usually the keeper.

## Video Generation Translation

Direct as two shots plus an edit point: Shot A ends holding the matched element stable ≥0.5s; hard cut; Shot B starts with identical element position, then motion resumes. Declare what matches (property + position + size) and what breaks (everything else). No dissolve, no morph — the seam IS the device.

## Failure Modes

- Morphing instead of cutting: generation blends A into B continuously → enforce hard cut semantics; the trick dies without the seam.
- Position mismatch after cut (element jumps) → state exact screen coordinates ("element centered, upper third boundary") for both sides.
- Double match confusion (shape AND color AND motion all change target) → lock one primary property per cut.
- Motif unrecognizable post-transform → keep silhouette class consistent across the transform.

## Preservation Rules

The surviving element is sacred: its recognizable geometry may not drift across the cut. Face identity never rides the match (a face may appear in B, but B's anchor is the object/shape). Brand marks: use logo geometry AS the matching shape deliberately, never incidentally. Spatial continuity resets at the cut by design — declare it, don't apologize. Motif link: this is the primary verb-Match carrier (motif-system-link.md); log every match in the sequence ledger to avoid repeating the same property twice consecutively.
