# Pass Through

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Pass Through
- **Source status:** taxonomy entry verified (category exists at slug); page definition not captured in this build — identity below is derived naming support.
- **Visual purpose:** camera (or subject) passes through an intervening layer — crowd, fabric, glass, foliage, doorway — and the world on the other side is different; the wipe hides the cut or the location change.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Spatial expansion: one continuous move must open a second space without a visible cut.
- Transition: scene change disguised inside foreground business (subject crosses frame behind glass; emerges elsewhere).
- Tension: something approaches through partial concealment — seen, then seen clearly.
- Product film: camera travels across/through product surfaces into macro detail.

## Avoid When

- The occluding layer completely swallows the subject for long durations (audience loses the thread).
- The layer's texture fights the subject's read (busy pattern + small face = mush).
- Continuity matters more than surprise: pass-through resets geography by design.
- Used repeatedly within one sequence as the only transition type.

## Required Inputs

- Occluding layer with a passable gap/path (doorway, parted fabric, gap in crowd, window pane for lens-pass).
- Destination world staged behind it, differing in at least two properties (palette, depth, scale).
- Pass axis: straight-through vs angled sweep.
- What leads the pass: camera alone, subject first, or object-first.

## Directing Rule

- Occluder occupies near plane, subject mid, destination far — three declared depth lanes.
- Partial occlusion ceiling: the layer may cover ≤60% of frame at maximum; beyond that, orientation is lost.
- Pass mechanics: leading edge of subject/camera enters the gap; during crossing, destination world becomes visible THROUGH the gap before we fully commit — anticipation frame.
- Lighting shift at crossing: destination world announces itself with its own light temperature/direction as the layer slides past.
- Speed: steady approach, brief deceleration at threshold, resume — the "breath" makes the reveal deliberate rather than accidental.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** occluder established WITH its context (we know what door/fabric/crowd this is); subject approaching from readable distance.
- **Transition frame:** mid-crossing — frame split between leaving-world sliver and arriving-world majority; contact edges crisp.
- **Reveal frame:** destination world full-frame; the occluder now exists only as an exit element behind.

## Image Generation Translation

Compose at the threshold: frame divided by the occluder edge — roughly 1/3 old world, 2/3 new world — with the subject at the boundary, half-lit by each side. This single still sells both worlds plus the motion implied. Keep subject identity features fully visible (do not let the layer cross the face).

## Video Generation Translation

One continuous move along the pass axis. Declare: layer material and behavior (parts, slides, static gap), approach speed, threshold pause (~0.3s), exit speed, and lighting handover point (where new light takes over). Lock: subject scale in frame, screen direction unchanged across the pass. No internal cut; if generation cannot hold continuity, split into two shots AT maximum occlusion (the layer covers the seam).

## Failure Modes

- Subject geometry smears during crossing (fast relative motion) → slow the pass; increase layer clearance around face/product.
- Layer behaves like liquid instead of solid (fabric turns to fog) → name the material and its physics explicitly.
- Destination leaks too early (new world visible from setup) → stage occluder opacity: nothing shows until threshold frame.
- Continuity jump: subject's wardrobe/lighting flips mid-pass → declare wardrobe state and both light sources before directing motion.

## Preservation Rules

Face never fully covered at any single instant; logo surfaces route around the layer, not through it. Wardrobe and held props persist across the boundary. Screen direction preserved: enter left-of-gap → exit left-of-center keeps geography honest. Motif link: pass-through carries verb-Occlusion — the motif may BE the occluder returning to hide-and-reveal. Downgrade path: hard cut hidden at peak occlusion (single black-ish frame) when true continuity fails.
