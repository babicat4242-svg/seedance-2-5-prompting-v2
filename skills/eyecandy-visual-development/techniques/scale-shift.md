# Scale Shift

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Scale Shift
- **Source status:** page-definition. Official site definition (verbatim): "Using forced perspective to create an illusion of scale, making small things look big, and big things look miniature."
- **Sub-types (verified):** Big head · Giant · Miniature
- **Visual purpose:** breaks the audience's size assumptions using forced perspective — a person towers over a city or drowns in a teacup.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Scale-perception: product must feel monumental (perfume bottle as architecture).
- Visual hook: impossible size pairing in frame one guarantees attention.
- Climax: subject reaches Giant scale against the world that previously dwarfed them.
- Whimsy/commerce balance: Miniature subjects inside everyday objects read playful, premium-craft.

## Avoid When

- Product truth: misleading size claims in advertising contexts — if the ad states real dimensions elsewhere, forced perspective must not contradict legal copy.
- Scene needs grounded realism; one scale trick poisons the documentary tone.
- Two different scale logics in the same shot without hierarchy.
- Subject identity relies on realistic body proportions (Big head distorts faces).

## Required Inputs

- Hero subject (the one whose size is lied about) and its true-scale reference.
- Reference objects with universally known sizes (doors, chairs, coins, hands).
- Direction: enlarging (subject grows relative to world) or shrinking (world swallows subject).
- Depth staging: foreground/midground/background lanes for the forced-perspective alignment.

## Directing Rule

- Place hero and reference on DIFFERENT depth planes; align their silhouettes along the lens axis so overlap is unambiguous at the contact point.
- Lock camera height and focal length conceptually wide (deep focus): both planes sharp, or the trick reads as blur error.
- Contact logic: where hero "touches" the far object, edges must kiss precisely — a 5% misalignment kills the illusion.
- Movement rule: move the CAMERA or the FAR plane only. The near hero stays locked during the reveal, or alignment collapses.
- Sub-type mapping: Big head = oversized near-plane head vs normal body behind; Giant = hero spans multiple background stories; Miniature = hero fits inside a handheld object.

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** establish TRUE scale first — hero among normal references, so the audience calibrates.
- **Transition frame:** the alignment moment — hero and far-reference silhouettes touching, sizes now ambiguous.
- **Reveal frame:** full impossibility visible (Giant straddles skyline / Miniature stands on a fingernail); one familiar anchor remains for scale reading.

## Image Generation Translation

Single-frame job: deep-focus composition, hero plane and reference plane both crisp, silhouette contact precise, at least one known-size anchor in frame. Wide-angle feel exaggerates near/far divergence. Avoid shallow depth of field entirely — bokeh destroys forced perspective.

## Video Generation Translation

Declare two depth planes and their contents, the alignment axis, and which plane moves: typically camera slides laterally or dollies while hero stays fixed, making the far world swell or shrink. Speed: slow enough to track the contact point. End state must hold ≥1s so the new scale registers. If transitioning from true scale, use a cut or whip to re-stage rather than morphing sizes continuously.

## Failure Modes

- Alignment drift mid-shot (planes slide out of register) → minimize movement duration; re-declare contact point in prompt.
- Focus system picks one plane and blurs the other → explicitly demand deep focus / both-sharp rendering.
- Size ambiguity: no known-size anchor → always include one door/hand/coin-class object.
- Subject proportions leak (Giant looks like a normal person on a small set) → specify environmental scale cues (windows, cars) at hero's scale.

## Preservation Rules

Face identity: Big head/Giant distort body scale but facial FEATURES stay canonical — never let the shift redraw identity. Product form/label: when the product is the giant or miniature, its proportions remain internally exact; only context lies. Wardrobe continuity across scale cuts. Spatial continuity: declare the lie's internal logic and keep it consistent for the whole sequence. Motif link: Scale Shift is the verb-Scale-change carrier (motif-system-link.md); motif travels the ladder object → costume → room → architecture through successive shifts. Downgrade path: replace forced perspective with a match-cut between true-scale framings.
