# Arc

<!-- SOURCE-GROUNDED -->
## Technique Identity

- **EYECANDY category:** Arc
- **Source status:** page-definition. Official site definition (verbatim): "The arc movement shot is like a choreographed dance, but with a camera. It's when you move the camera in a smooth, circular motion around your subject, creating a sense of elegance and sophistication…"
- **Sub-types (verified):** Arc spin · X-Axis Arc · Y-Axis Arc
- **Visual purpose:** orbiting the subject on a curved path — the world rotates around a stable center while the subject stays anchored.

<!-- DERIVED -->
## Use When

- Hero moment for a person or product that must stay iconic while the environment reveals itself behind it.
- Elegance: luxury, fashion, premium product — one unbroken graceful path.
- Escalation into a spin: energy build where rotation speed itself carries intensity (Arc spin).
- X-Axis Arc when background parallax should do the storytelling; Y-Axis Arc when vertical dominance (looking up/down) is the point.

## Avoid When

- Subject geometry unstable in generation (faces/labels warp on view change) — arc shows every side of the subject.
- Scene needs forward drive or urgency; arcs feel ceremonial, not urgent.
- Empty background: orbit without parallax content is a meaningless carousel.
- Already used an orbit in the previous two shots.

## Required Inputs

- Center of rotation (subject or subject-locked point).
- Orbit radius relative to subject size (tight = intimacy/power, wide = context).
- Start and end azimuth (how many degrees of travel).
- Background depth content at multiple distances (parallax fuel).
- Sub-type choice: horizontal orbit / X-axis (tilting rise-fall) / Y-axis (vertical sweep).

## Directing Rule

- Subject occupies frame center or golden-section anchor through the entire path; camera looks inward at all times.
- Constant radius, constant height unless the sub-type says otherwise: X-Axis Arc adds vertical sine motion, Y-Axis Arc travels a vertical half-circle (low-angle start → high-angle end or reverse).
- Speed profile: ease-in, constant middle, ease-out. Arc spin replaces ease-out with acceleration to full revolution.
- Keep one landmark object visible per 90° of travel so rotation reads against the environment, not against nothing.
- Horizon behavior must be declared: level horizon throughout (elegant) vs. tilting horizon (disorientation — that becomes a different intent).

## Storyboard Translation

- **Setup frame:** subject centered, first azimuth chosen so the most iconic face/side faces camera; background landmark A visible left.
- **Transition frame (mid-orbit):** subject same size and position, background now landmark B — the proof-of-rotation frame.
- **Reveal frame (end azimuth):** new background composition behind unchanged subject; if Arc spin, end frame returns to start azimuth with changed lighting/state.

## Image Generation Translation

Stills cannot show the path — imply it: choose mid-orbit framing where foreground element (shoulder, pillar, product edge) intrudes on one frame edge, background recedes opposite. Radial blur cues only if motion emphasis matters. The still must read as "camera caught mid-circuit": subject locked, world sweeping.

## Video Generation Translation

Specify: orbit direction (clockwise/counterclockwise), degrees of travel, radius as multiple of subject width, height behavior per sub-type, speed profile. Lock: subject scale in frame, focus distance. State what changes behind: background order (landmark A → B → C). For Arc spin declare full 360° and end-state match to start. One continuous take; no internal cuts.

## Failure Modes

- Meaningless orbit: background lacks depth layers → pre-declare three background planes before directing the arc.
- Subject drift from center (camera wobbles off axis) → restate lock: "subject fixed at frame center, camera maintains equal distance."
- Face/label distortion as viewing angle changes → limit travel to ≤120° for identity-critical subjects; use two shorter arcs across cuts instead.
- Arc spin nausea: full revolutions faster than ~2s disorient → keep spins ≥2s or hide cut at 180°.

## Preservation Rules

Face/product identity outranks orbital grace: cap angle travel so recognizable surfaces stay recognizable, or split the orbit across cuts preserving screen direction. Logo-bearing sides get dedicated frames, not incidental pass-bys. Spatial continuity: landmarks' relative positions must stay consistent across the whole orbit — the world rotates coherently. Motif link: place the active motif as one of the background landmarks so the arc sweeps past it (verb: Match).
